Listening A[i]gent – Organizational Signal & Feedback

Turning qualitative noise into structured insight · by Diane Wilkinson

Capture real signals without surveys nobody trusts.

Listening A[i]gent structures feedback already present in everyday people workflows — interviews, 1:1s, retros, notes, handoffs — into a consistent signal layer with attribution and context preserved.

It is designed to be non-invasive and human-in-the-loop: no performative “listening,” no black-box sentiment, and no anonymous dashboards divorced from action.

Who this is for

People Ops, HRBPs, TA Ops, and People Analytics teams who want early warning signals and decision-ready insight — without turning “listening” into performative surveys or anonymous scoreboards.

1. Problem & Purpose

1.1 The Problem

Most organizations believe they are “listening,” but rely on:

  • Low-response engagement surveys
  • Anonymous comments with no ownership
  • One-off feedback that never loops back
  • Sentiment dashboards divorced from decisions

The result is signal loss, trust erosion, and leadership blind spots.

1.2 Purpose

Listening A[i]gent creates a structured signal layer from inputs already present in day-to-day work — preserving attribution and context so teams can act.

Principle: Listening only “counts” when feedback becomes action, and action becomes visible.

2. Scope & Design Principles

2.1 In-Scope

  • Structured capture of qualitative feedback from existing workflows
  • Themes, risks, friction points, and “what changed” timelines
  • Decision-ready summaries for Ops/HR leadership
  • Closed-loop tracking: captured → triaged → actioned → communicated

2.2 Out-of-Scope (v1)

  • Fully automated sentiment scoring without context
  • Anonymous leaderboards or performance management scoring
  • Open-ended surveillance (e.g., always-on chat monitoring)

2.3 Design Principles

  • Non-invasive. Use existing artifacts; avoid “extra asks” that drive fatigue.
  • Human-in-the-loop. Humans confirm meaning; AI helps structure and summarize.
  • Attribution preserved. Anonymous inputs only when necessary and explicitly justified.
  • Action linkage. Every insight must map to an owner, a decision, or a next step.
  • Auditable. Preserve sources, dates, and decision notes.

2.4 Guardrails

  • Clear separation between signal, hypothesis, and conclusion.
  • No “AI scoring” used for employee performance decisions.
  • Minimum aggregation thresholds before org-level trend reporting.
  • Access controls: sensitive signals visible only to appropriate roles.

3. Roles & Responsibilities

Role Responsibilities
People Ops / HRBP Owns signal triage, assigns follow-up owners, ensures closed-loop communication.
People Analytics Maintains taxonomy, validates trends, builds reporting for leadership (with guardrails).
Managers Participate in follow-ups, clarify context, implement local fixes, communicate actions.
Employees Provide feedback through normal workflows (1:1s, retros, notes). No extra burden required.
System Admin (HRIS/Tools) Sets up capture points, permissions, retention rules, and logging.

4. System Overview

Listening A[i]gent has four core capability areas:

  • Capture – collect signals from existing artifacts (notes, comments, scorecards, retros).
  • Structure – normalize into a taxonomy (theme, risk, severity, confidence, owner).
  • Triage – route to the right owner with suggested next steps.
  • Close the loop – track actions and communicate “what changed.”

4.1 Inputs (Examples)

  • Interview feedback + scorecard comments
  • Recruiter / HRBP notes
  • Manager 1:1 summaries
  • Exit and transition summaries
  • Operational retrospectives

4.2 Outputs

  • Thematic summaries tied to specific workflows
  • Early-warning indicators (friction, burnout, confusion)
  • Decision-ready insight for Ops and leadership
  • Documented feedback loops showing “what changed”

5. Signal Taxonomy

Signals should be structured consistently so teams can compare apples to apples.

Field Description Example Values
theme High-level topic area Role clarity, workload, tools, manager quality, process friction
signal_type What kind of feedback this is Friction, risk, suggestion, praise, confusion, policy gap
severity Potential impact if true Low / Medium / High
confidence How certain we are Low / Medium / High (based on corroboration)
source Where it came from 1:1 note, interview feedback, retro doc, exit summary
attribution How identity is handled Named / role-level / anonymized
owner Who must respond HRBP, manager, Ops, IT, leadership
status Closed-loop stage Captured → Triaged → In progress → Resolved → Communicated
Rule: A signal without an owner is just noise.

6. Capture & Workflow Patterns

6.1 Lightweight Capture (Best Default)

  • Add a simple “Signal tags” section to existing docs/templates (retro notes, 1:1 prompts).
  • Allow quick tagging: theme + severity + suggested owner.
  • Weekly triage by People Ops/HRBP.

6.2 Structured Capture Points

ID Capture Point Trigger Output
L1 Exit / transition summary Exit initiated Top 3 themes + actionable owners
L2 Manager 1:1 summary Weekly/biweekly cadence Friction + risk flags for triage
L3 Retro notes Project milestone Process / tooling issues with timestamps
L4 Interview feedback (candidate experience) Interview completed CX friction themes + ownership

6.3 Triage Loop

  • Weekly: HRBP/Ops reviews new signals and assigns owners.
  • Biweekly: validate repeat signals (raise confidence if corroborated).
  • Monthly: leadership summary of top themes + actions shipped.

7. Implementation Notes

7.1 Where It Lives

  • Docs-first: simplest — tags inside existing notes and templates.
  • System-first: structured form fields in HRIS/ATS (where appropriate).
  • Hybrid: tags in docs + periodic structured rollup.

7.2 Access & Retention

  • Define a retention policy for raw notes vs. aggregated insights.
  • Restrict access to named attribution; share trends at aggregate level.
  • Log who viewed or exported sensitive summaries.
Default recommendation: Start docs-first for 30–45 days, prove value, then add structure.

8. Metrics & Reporting

Measure whether listening becomes action:

  • Time from signal captured → owner assigned
  • Time from owner assigned → action shipped
  • % of signals with closed-loop communication
  • Repeat-signal rate by theme (is the system improving?)
  • Top friction themes by org / role family (with thresholds)

8.1 Integration with Metrics A[i]gent

Listening signals can be mapped to a shared metrics layer as event objects: signal_captured, signal_triaged, action_shipped, loop_closed.

9. Fit in the A[i]gents Suite

  • Upstream: Workflow A[i]gent and other systems generate operational artifacts where signals live.
  • Core: Listening A[i]gent structures feedback into a consistent taxonomy and triage loop.
  • Downstream: Innovation A[i]gent can pick up systemic issues as experiment candidates; Metrics A[i]gent reports progress.

10. Risks & Mitigations

  • Risk: “Listening” becomes surveillance.
    Mitigation: non-invasive inputs, clear boundaries, access controls.
  • Risk: Anonymous dumping ground with no action.
    Mitigation: ownership required, closed-loop metrics.
  • Risk: Misinterpretation without context.
    Mitigation: human review, preserve sources, avoid black-box scoring.
  • Risk: Leaders use signals for performance scoring.
    Mitigation: policy guardrails + audit trail + restricted outputs.
