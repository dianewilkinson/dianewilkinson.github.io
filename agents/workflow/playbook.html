1. Problem & Outcomes

1.1 The Problem

Most interview processes silently leak:

  • Candidates no-show or drop after scheduling.
  • Interviewers don’t submit scorecards on time.
  • Hiring managers forget to deliver feedback.
  • Candidates get inconsistent instructions across stages.
  • Recruiters manually chase everyone, all the time.

This creates longer time-to-fill, slower decisions, inconsistent experience, and constant operational drag.

1.2 The Outcomes We’re Aiming For

  • Clean, predictable interview flows from screen → offer.
  • Scorecards submitted within agreed-upon SLAs.
  • Fewer no-shows and fewer “I never heard back” complaints.
  • Standard, role-appropriate candidate communications.
  • Recruiters focusing on real conversations instead of inbox herding.

2. Scope & Design Principles

2.1 In-Scope

  • Phone screen → HM/Tech → Panel → Final → Offer → Pre-start.
  • Candidate, interviewer, and hiring manager communications.
  • Time-based triggers and SLA logic.
  • Email and calendar template library.
  • ATS-native automation rules and lightweight orchestration.

2.2 Out-of-Scope (v1)

  • Automated note-taking or transcription.
  • LLM-generated feedback summaries.
  • Candidate sourcing or nurture campaigns.
  • Post-hire onboarding workflows.

2.3 Design Principles

  • Candidate-first. Clear expectations, no surprises.
  • Low lift. Built on native ATS features wherever possible.
  • Observable. Every automation leaves a trail in the ATS timeline.
  • Configurable. Stages and templates can vary by role and region.
  • Fail-safe. Humans can always override or pause automations.

2.4 Global Rules

  • Use the candidate’s local time in all scheduling messages; show both candidate and host time zones.
  • Always include self-service reschedule / cancel options where the stack supports it.
  • Ask about accommodations in every invite and include a phone backup.
  • Do not include sensitive data in calendar bodies; link to secure docs instead.
  • Standardize subject formats: [Company] · [Role] · [Stage].

4. System Overview

At a high level, Workflow A[i]gent delivers four major capability areas:

  • Scheduling workflows – availability capture, reminders if candidates don’t respond, and alerts when threads stall.
  • Stage-based interview workflows – confirmations, reminders, and follow-ups for each interview stage.
  • SLAs & escalations – scorecard due dates, automatic nudges, and escalation paths when people are late.
  • Status & delay updates – structured check-ins when things are taking longer than usual.

4.1 ATS Implementation (Conceptual)

The playbook assumes a modern ATS (such as Greenhouse or Lever) with the ability to:

  • Trigger emails or tasks when stages change.
  • Reference reusable templates for different offices and departments.
  • Fire rules based on “time since event” (e.g., X hours after interview end).

Where the ATS cannot handle a pattern natively, a lightweight orchestration layer (Zapier, Make, internal microservice) can bridge the gap using webhooks and APIs.

5. Workflow Library

This library captures the core flows most teams need. Each can be adapted to your stages and naming.

5.1 Trigger Glossary

  • Stage Change – triggered whenever a candidate moves into a new interview stage.
  • Event Booked – triggered when an interview calendar event is created.
  • Time-Based – triggered relative to interview start, interview end, or amount of time in stage.
  • Manual Override – recruiter-triggered emails for exceptional or sensitive situations.

5.2 Scheduling & Availability

Goal: reduce drop-off between “stage change” and “interview booked.”

ID Trigger Recipient Description
S1 Stage → Initial Screen Candidate Send availability request with basic expectations and scheduling link.
S2 No reply after 48h Candidate Polite follow-up reminding them to book.
S3 No reply after 96h Recruiter Alert to review candidate and decide whether to move on, nudge, or close out.
S4 Stage → HM / Tech / Final Candidate Role-specific availability request and prep guidance.
S5 Event booked (any stage) Candidate Confirmation with calendar details and expectations.

5.3 Interview Stage Workflows

For each stage (Screen, HM/Tech, Panel, Final), the pattern is:

  • Confirm – send immediately when the interview is booked.
  • Remind – send roughly 24 hours before.
  • Reinforce – optional same-day reminder, especially for multi-part panels.
  • Follow-up – send 2–4 hours after to thank and set expectations.
  • Scorecard nudges – to interviewers until feedback is in.

Example: Initial Screen

ID Trigger Recipient Action
I1 Event booked → Initial Screen Candidate Confirmation with recruiter intro, format, and prep tips.
I2 24h before Candidate Reminder with time zone, join link, and any homework.
I3 2h after Candidate Follow-up with “thanks for your time” and next-step expectations.
I4 Stage change → Removed Candidate Regret email tailored to this stage.

Example: HM / Technical Interview

ID Trigger Recipient Action
T1 Event booked Candidate Confirmation with what will be assessed and who they’ll meet.
T2 24h before Candidate Reminder with logistics and expectations.
T3 2h after Candidate Follow-up acknowledging their time and giving timing for next steps.
T4 24h before Interviewer Reminder to review the profile, rubric, and scorecard structure.
T5 2h after Interviewer First scorecard nudge.
T6 24h after Interviewer + HM Second nudge and notification to HM that feedback is overdue.

Example: Final Interview follows the same pattern but with more context about decision-making and timelines.

5.4 Scorecard & SLA Automation

Objective: keep feedback fresh and avoid stalled candidates.

  • +2h after interview – first reminder to submit scorecard.
  • +24h after – second reminder plus ping to hiring manager.
  • +48h after – escalation to recruiting lead and HM’s manager.
  • Optional: block progression to the next stage until all required scorecards are complete.

5.5 Status & Delay Updates

Objective: keep candidates informed when internal timelines slip.

  • Structured “we’re still actively interviewing” notes when panels or debriefs take longer than expected.
  • Templates for roles on hold, hiring manager travel, or shifting priorities.
  • Optional “what’s happening behind the scenes” messages for senior or executive candidates.

5.6 Offer & Pre-Start Workflows

  • Offer scheduling link or direct coordination from the recruiter.
  • Offer call confirmation with agenda.
  • Post-acceptance email covering paperwork, IT, and first-day expectations.
  • Reminder 1–2 weeks before start with logistics and any prep materials.

6. Configuration & Implementation Notes

Typical implementation steps:

  • Map current stages → Screen, HM/Tech, Panel, Final, Offer.
  • Define SLAs for each stage (scorecards, response times, etc.).
  • Create a template library (confirmation, reminder, follow-up, delay, regret).
  • Wire triggers using ATS automation features:
    • On stage change
    • On event booked
    • On time since interview
  • Pilot on 1–2 roles (e.g., Sales + Eng) before expanding.
  • Train recruiters, coordinators, and HMs on what’s automated vs. manual.
  • Monitor metrics & tune: no-show rate, SLA adherence, average days per stage.

6.1 Role-Based Customization

Most teams need at least light customization by role family:

  • Sales – heavier pre-interview prep, panel logistics, and expectation setting.
  • Engineering – linkage to assessments, code samples, or portfolio reviews.
  • G&A – typically fewer stages and faster response expectations.
  • Leadership – more stakeholders, tailored messaging, and longer decision cycles.

6.2 Example ATS Rules

  • When candidate enters “Recruiter Screen” → send initial scheduling email.
  • If no activity after 48h in “Interview” stage → send delay update.
  • When interview ends → schedule scorecard reminders at +2h and +24h.
  • If scorecards incomplete at 48h → add task to HM and ping recruiting lead.

7. Metrics & Reporting

Workflow A[i]gent is designed to be measured. Suggested metrics include:

  • No-show rate by stage.
  • Average days in each interview stage.
  • Percentage of scorecards submitted within SLA.
  • Candidate satisfaction (CSAT / NPS) where collected.
  • Template usage rate versus ad-hoc emails.
  • Volume of delay updates sent and roles covered.

7.1 Integration with the Metrics Dictionary

Workflow A[i]gent emits standardized timestamps and event objects that map to the shared Metrics Dictionary fields. This allows Metrics A[i]gent to calculate:

  • Time in stage and total time-to-offer.
  • Scorecard SLA adherence.
  • Interview-to-decision velocity.
  • Candidate friction (days between interviews).
  • Pass-through rates across Screen → Panel → Offer.

Example event mappings:

Workflow Event Metrics Dictionary Field Used For
stage_entered_at stage_start_time Time-in-stage calculations
interview_scheduled_at interview_scheduled_time SLA tracking, throughput
interview_start_at interview_start_time Wait time and friction analysis

scorecard_submitted_at scorecard_submitted_time SLA compliance
offer_extended_at offer_extended_time Time-to-offer
delay_update_sent_at delay_update_sent_time Delay coverage & CX metrics

8. Future Enhancements (AI-Forward Roadmap)

  • Use LLMs to generate delay updates and candidate check-ins from structured prompts and guardrails.
  • Automatically summarize interviews (via an Interview A[i]gent) and attach structured notes to scorecards.
  • Dynamically choose templates based on candidate risk (e.g., critical hires, late-stage executive candidates).
  • Proactive SLA risk alerts in Slack / Teams when interviews, scorecards, or offers are trending late.
  • “Interview quality” analytics across interviewers and stages to inform training and calibration.

9. How This Fits in the A[i]gents Suite

Workflow A[i]gent sits in the middle of the recruiting lifecycle:

  • Upstream: Screening A[i]gent evaluates inbound applicants and decides who should move into interviews.
  • Core: Workflow A[i]gent orchestrates all interview-stage communications, SLAs, and status updates.
  • Downstream: Metrics A[i]gent ingests clean events and timestamps to compute time-to-fill, pass-through, and SLA performance. A future Interview A[i]gent can supply structured notes into scorecards.

Together they form an AI-ready, automation-first recruiting operating system.

10. Risks & Mitigations

  • Risk: Over-automation makes the process feel cold or robotic.
    Mitigation: Reserve key moments for human outreach; keep templates warm and personalized; allow recruiters to pause flows.
  • Risk: Incorrect triggers send the wrong communication.
    Mitigation: Test with dummy candidates in a sandbox; roll out in phases; log every automation.
  • Risk: Hiring managers ignore SLAs.
    Mitigation: Add escalation paths, publish SLA reports, and make scorecard compliance visible in performance conversations.
  • Risk: Workflows become stale as the business changes.
    Mitigation: Review metrics quarterly, adjust templates and SLAs, and keep a lightweight change-log.
