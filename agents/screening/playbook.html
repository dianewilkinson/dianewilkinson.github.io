1. Problem & Mission

1.1 The Problem

At scale, most screening processes quietly optimize for speed over judgement:

  • Recruiters skim resumes for 6–10 seconds due to workload.
  • Top candidates get buried under noisy ATS scores and keyword stuffing.
  • Different recruiters apply different mental rubrics to the same role.
  • Reasons for rejection are rarely structured or auditable.
  • Funnel data is incomplete, making it hard to debug quality & fairness issues later.

The result is the opposite of candidate excellence: inconsistency, bias, hidden talent going unnoticed, and confusing signals for hiring managers.

1.2 Mission

Screening A[i]gent exists to give every application a consistent deep dive in seconds, not minutes — and to rescue high-potential talent others miss — while keeping recruiters firmly in control of the final decision.

1.3 Outcomes We’re Aiming For

  • Fair, rubric-driven evaluation for every resume.
  • Clear, explainable recommendations for recruiters & HMs.
  • Structured data that plugs directly into Workflow and Metrics A[i]gents.
  • Less time on “obvious no” reviews; more time on conversation and calibration.

2. Scope & Design Principles

2.1 In-Scope

  • Inbound application / resume evaluation for defined roles.
  • Transformation of JDs and HM requirements into role-specific rubrics.
  • Hybrid scoring across experience, tenure, employer fit, skills, education, and soft indicators.
  • Risk flagging (e.g., job hopping, gaps, inconsistent titles).
  • Recommendations: Advance / HM Review / Do Not Advance.
  • Ask-next prompts for recruiter screens.
  • Structured outputs into ATS fields and Metrics A[i]gent.

2.2 Out-of-Scope (v1)

  • Live interview workflows (owned by Workflow A[i]gent).
  • Offer, onboarding, or HRIS provisioning.
  • Sourcing, campaign management, or nurture flows.
  • Full ML-based prediction of performance (kept as a future, grounded extension).

2.3 Design Principles

  • Rubric-first, model-second. The rubric is the contract; AI is the assistant.
  • Hybrid scoring. Every category has a human-readable band and a numeric score under the hood.
  • Explainable. Every recommendation can be traced to specific signals and weights.
  • Human-in-the-loop. Recruiters must approve or override the agent’s recommendation.
  • Bias-aware. Flags, not vetoes; calibration over time, not hard-coded stereotypes.

2.4 Global Rules

  • No fully automated rejection: a human must confirm “Do Not Advance.”
  • Every override requires a short note, creating an audit trail.
  • Rubrics and weights are documented and discoverable by TA & HMs.
  • The agent does not infer or store protected characteristic data.

4. System Overview

At a high level, Screening A[i]gent runs the following loop:

  • 1. Inputs: JD + HM priorities + resume + basic metadata (location, source, etc.).
  • 2. Rubric assembly: Convert requirements into structured rubric components.
  • 3. Evidence extraction: Parse resume and profile for roles, companies, tenure, skills, education, and signals.
  • 4. Scoring: Apply hybrid scoring across categories, with risk penalties.
  • 5. Recommendation: Generate recommended disposition and ask-next prompts.
  • 6. Output: Write scores, bands, tags, and recommendation back to ATS fields for humans and Metrics A[i]gent.

This playbook defines the rubric and scoring logic; the implementation can be done in any modern stack (LLM + orchestration + ATS integration).

5. Rubric & Signal Library

The rubric is broken out into categories with both band-level interpretation and numeric scores. Risk flags act as negative adjustments to the overall score.

5.1 Rubric Categories

Category Max Points Summary
Experience Depth 30 Relevancy of prior roles to this job’s scope and level.
Tenure Stability 15 Consistency and average time-in-role, tuned for tech.
Competitor / Industry Fit 15 Employer pedigree across competitors, adjacencies, and relevant tech.
Skills & Tools Match 25 Coverage of must-have and nice-to-have skills; transferability.
Education & Credentials 5 Baseline requirements and relevant advanced credentials.
Soft Indicators 10 Signals like clarity, detail, follow-through, and level of polish.
Risk Flags (penalty) -20 Moderate negative weighting for meaningful risk patterns.

5.2 Experience Depth – Signals

  • High band: 3+ years in highly relevant roles, clearly progressive scope.
  • Medium band: 1–3 years relevant, plus some adjacent or transferable experience.
  • Low band: Mostly unrelated roles, unclear match to job level or domain.

5.3 Tenure Stability – Tech-Tuned

Tech markets skew more dynamic, so expectations are adjusted accordingly:

  • High band: average 2–3 years per role, some longer stints, reasonable moves.
  • Medium band: average around 1.5–2 years, a few shorter roles but overall coherent.
  • Low band: repeated sub-12-month roles, unexplained gaps, or frequent lateral moves without progression.

5.4 Competitor / Industry Fit – Generous Model

  • High band: direct competitors, adjacent companies in the same ecosystem, or strong, well-known tech brands.
  • Medium band: general tech companies, relevant adjacent industries, or strong brands outside the core ecosystem.
  • Low band: low-signal employers for this role (completely different domain, no clear transferrable context).

5.5 Skills & Tools Match

  • High band: all must-have skills present and demonstrated; several nice-to-haves.
  • Medium band: most must-haves, strong transferable skills, some nice-to-haves missing.
  • Low band: missing critical skills or only adjacent experience without clear transfer.

5.6 Education & Credentials

  • High band: required degree plus directly relevant advanced study / certifications.
  • Medium band: required or equivalent; relevant coursework or partial credentials.
  • Low band: missing non-negotiable requirements (where they are truly business-critical).

5.7 Soft Indicators

  • Clarity and structure of resume content.
  • Evidence of results (metrics, impact, ownership).
  • Consistency between roles, responsibilities, and claimed achievements.

5.8 Risk Flags (Moderate Penalty)

Risk flags do not automatically disqualify a candidate; they trigger ask-next questions and moderate penalties.

  • Repeated short tenures (< 12 months) without obvious context.
  • Unexplained multi-year gaps.
  • Title inflation vs scope (e.g., “VP” for an IC-level role).
  • Overly generic or buzzword-heavy content with little evidence of outcomes.

6. Hybrid Scoring & Recommendations

The hybrid model combines banded scores (for explainability) with numeric ranges (for analytics and tuning).

6.1 Category Bands & Points

Category Band Points (example)
Experience Depth High / Med / Low 24–30 / 16–23 / 0–15
Tenure Stability High / Med / Low 12–15 / 8–11 / 0–7
Competitor Fit High / Med / Low 12–15 / 7–11 / 0–6
Skills Match High / Med / Low 20–25 / 12–19 / 0–11
Education High / Med / Low 4–5 / 2–3 / 0–1
Soft Indicators High / Med / Low 8–10 / 4–7 / 0–3
Risk Flags None / Mild / Significant 0 / -5 / -10 to -20

6.2 Overall Score Bands

  • 90–100: Strong Match
  • 75–89: Solid Match
  • 60–74: Partial Match
  • < 60: Weak Match

6.3 Recommendation Logic

The recommendation is based on score + must-have requirements + risk flags:

  • Advance:
    • Overall score ≥ 80,
    • All must-have requirements met,
    • No “significant” (high-penalty) risk flags.
  • HM Review:
    • Score between ~65–79, or
    • Mixed signal (e.g., strong experience but some risk flags), or
    • Unusual, non-linear background with potential upside.
  • Do Not Advance:
    • Missing a non-negotiable requirement and overall score below threshold, or
    • Very low overall score (< ~65) with significant risk patterns.

The agent always emits both the band (“Strong Match”) and the numeric score (e.g., 86/100), plus a plain-language explanation summarizing why.

7. Ask-Next Prompts & HM Summaries

Recommendations are not the end of the story; they drive better conversations. Screening A[i]gent proposes:

7.1 Ask-Next for Recruiter Screens

  • Experience depth: “Walk me through your work on X; what were you directly responsible for?”
  • Tenure: “I noticed a few shorter roles in [years]. Can you share the context?”
  • Skills: “Tell me about a recent project where you used [tool/skill] end-to-end.”
  • Risk flags: “I see a gap between [year] and [year]. What were you focused on then?”

7.2 HM Preview Summary

For candidates marked Advance or HM Review, the agent produces a short HM-facing summary:

  • 1–2 sentence overview of current/past roles and level.
  • Top 3 strengths relative to the rubric.
  • Top 1–2 concerns or open questions.
  • Overall band and score (e.g., “Solid Match – 82/100”).

These summaries help HMs calibrate more quickly, especially when they’re comparing multiple candidates surfaced by Screening A[i]gent.

8. Calibration & Governance

Rubrics are living documents; calibration makes them better over time.

8.1 Shadow Mode

  • Run the agent silently alongside manual recruiter decisions for an initial period.
  • Compare agent recommendations vs actual decisions.
  • Collect examples where recruiters disagree with the agent’s call.

8.2 Override Tracking

  • Every override (both “Advance anyway” and “Do Not Advance despite high score”) requires a short note.
  • Metrics A[i]gent tracks override rate by role, recruiter, and segment.
  • High override clusters signal rubric misalignment or training gaps.

8.3 Rubric Reviews

  • Quarterly reviews with TA Ops + HMs to adjust thresholds and weights.
  • Use real candidate samples where the rubric under- or overscored.
  • Update documentation and re-communicate any changes to recruiters.

8.4 Fairness & Bias Monitoring

  • Monitor pass-through and override rates across candidate groups.
  • Look for patterns where certain backgrounds are consistently scored lower.
  • Use findings to refine rubrics and ask-next prompts, not to hard-code new stereotypes.

9.2 Implementation Notes

  • Start with a minimal, stable field set; add more after adoption.
  • Hide internal-only fields from hiring managers if they add noise.
  • Ensure Screening A[i]gent writes to fields only after recruiter confirmation.

10. Metrics & Metrics A[i]gent Integration

Screening A[i]gent feeds Metrics A[i]gent, which powers your recruiting analytics.

10.1 Key Screening Metrics

  • Application → Screen pass-through (by source, role, recruiter).
  • Distribution of screening bands (how many Strong vs Partial, etc.).
  • Override rate and direction (Advance vs Do Not Advance overrides).
  • Time from application to screening decision.
  • Downstream performance: how often Strong / Solid matches reach late stages or offers.

10.2 Event Mapping

Taken together, these metrics help answer: “Is our screening rubric surfacing the right talent, fairly, and fast enough to meet hiring goals?”

Appendix A – Bands & Point Ranges

This appendix summarizes the hybrid band → points mapping for quick reference.

Band Description Typical Points (per category)
High Clear, strong alignment with rubric expectations. ≈ 80–100% of the max for that category.
Medium Good but not perfect alignment; some gaps or trade-offs. ≈ 50–79% of the max.
Low Limited alignment or significant missing ingredients. ≈ 0–49% of the max.
No Risk No meaningful risk flags detected. 0 penalty.
Mild Risk One or two flags with plausible explanations. ≈ -5 penalty.
Significant Risk Multiple or severe flags that warrant caution. ≈ -10 to -20 penalty.

Exact ranges can be tuned per company and role; the important part is that bands are stable, documented, and shared with stakeholders.

Appendix B – Example Role Profiles

These examples show how the rubric might be applied to different common roles.

B.1 AE / Account Executive

  • Experience: quota-carrying SaaS sales, similar ACV and sales cycle.
  • Tenure: 2–3 year stints; some startup volatility acceptable.
  • Competitor Fit: direct competitors, adjacent SaaS products, industry familiarity.
  • Skills: full-funnel sales motions, MEDDIC/BANT, CRM expertise.

B.2 SDR / BDR

  • Experience: outbound prospecting, high-volume outreach, pipeline generation.
  • Signals: activity-level metrics, conversion from meetings to pipeline.
  • Competitor Fit: tech sales, preferably similar ICP.

B.3 Recruiter / Talent Partner

  • Experience: end-to-end recruiting for similar roles and hiring managers.
  • Tenure: enough time in roles to show cycles and re-hire scenarios.
  • Skills: sourcing, stakeholder management, ATS usage, data hygiene.

B.4 Software Engineer

  • Experience: languages, frameworks, and types of systems that match the role.
  • Signals: shipped features, ownership of components, depth vs breadth.
  • Education: CS or equivalent, strong projects, open-source contributions.

In all cases, the rubric is tuned per job family, but the underlying hybrid model, scoring, and governance remain consistent.
