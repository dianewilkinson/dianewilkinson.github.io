Who this is for

TA leaders, recruiting ops, and tech recruiters who need structured sourcing patterns for engineering, data, and AI roles — without turning the process into a black box.

1. Problem & Mission

1.1 The Problem

Most tech sourcing starts with a vague request and a blank search bar:

  • Hiring managers say “we need great engineers” without a clear market view.
  • Recruiters start from scratch on every role, rebuilding Boolean and target lists.
  • Sourcing quality depends on personal intuition and bookmarks, not shared patterns.
  • Company mapping lives in scattered spreadsheets and individual memory.
  • Sourcing effort is hard to measure, and even harder to debug when pipelines dry up.

The result: inconsistent coverage of tech talent, hidden pockets of candidates, and a recruiting team that’s always “busy sourcing” but can’t clearly show what’s working.

1.2 Mission

Sourcing A[i]gent exists to give every tech search a structured starting point — market map, target companies, and Boolean patterns — in minutes instead of days, while keeping recruiters in control of who to approach and how.

1.3 Outcomes We’re Aiming For

  • Standard, reusable sourcing blueprints for common tech roles.
  • Faster time-to-first-outreach when a new role opens.
  • Clear visibility into which markets and companies we’re targeting — and why.
  • Structured sourcing data that feeds Screening, Workflow, and Metrics A[i]gents.

2. Scope & Design Principles

2.1 In-Scope

  • Engineering, data, ML / AI, DevOps, and security roles (v1 focus).
  • Translation of JDs and hiring briefs into sourcing-ready role profiles.
  • Generation of Boolean and X-ray patterns for LinkedIn, GitHub, and open web search.
  • Company mapping: competitors, adjacencies, and “hidden gem” sources.
  • High-level market sizing & pipeline readiness estimates (small / medium / large talent pools).
  • Structured outputs that plug into Screening and Metrics A[i]gents.

2.2 Out-of-Scope (v1)

  • Automated outreach, nurture, or sequencing (handled by future Campaign or CRM A[i]gents).
  • Replacing recruiter judgement on which candidates to contact.
  • Real-time scraping or user-level tracking of candidate behavior.
  • Predicting candidate response rates or close likelihood (beyond simple assumptions).

2.3 Design Principles

  • Pattern-first, tools-second. Sourcing logic should survive tool changes.
  • Explainable search logic. Every pattern can be read, edited, and taught to humans.
  • Tech-ecosystem aware. Roles are defined in context of real stacks and company types.
  • Human-in-the-loop. Recruiters approve, refine, and save reusable patterns.
  • Non-creepy by default. Focus on public professional data and market-level insights.

2.4 Global Rules

  • No automated candidate outreach without recruiter review and explicit consent from stakeholders.
  • No inference or storage of protected characteristics.
  • Company mapping can be shared with hiring managers; individual candidate data stays controlled.
  • All role templates and patterns are versioned and discoverable for TA teams.

Key idea: Sourcing A[i]gent does the heavy thinking on where and how to search; humans still decide which candidates to approach and how to tell the story.

4. System Overview

At a high level, Sourcing A[i]gent runs the following loop for each role:

  • 1. Inputs: JD, hiring brief, tech stack, locations, seniority, must-have vs nice-to-have.
  • 2. Role profile assembly: Convert requirements into a structured tech role template.
  • 3. Market mapping: Identify target companies and adjacent talent pools.
  • 4. Search strategy: Generate Boolean and X-ray strings for LinkedIn, GitHub, and web.
  • 5. Pipeline readiness: Rough sizing of talent pool and recommended sourcing channels.
  • 6. Output: Write role profile, search patterns, and company lists into a structured record that Screening and Metrics A[i]gents can use.

4.1 Technical Architecture

Under the hood, Sourcing A[i]gent uses:

  • A parsing layer (LLM + rules) to extract role attributes from JDs and hiring briefs.
  • A role template library for common tech roles (backend, ML, data, DevOps, security).
  • A pattern engine to assemble Boolean, X-ray, and GitHub search filters.
  • An optional company index (CSV / DB) for structured competitor and group mapping.
  • Simple integrations with ATS/CRM fields or internal sheets for saving and reusing patterns.

The same playbook can be implemented via Python notebooks, lightweight internal tools, or as a back-end service behind a simple recruiter-facing UI.

5. Inputs & Signal Library

Before we generate any search patterns, the agent needs a structured view of the role. Inputs are deliberately lightweight so they fit into a live intake with a hiring manager.

5.1 Core Inputs

Field Description
role_title Label used internally (e.g., “Senior Backend Engineer,” “Staff ML Engineer”).
role_family Engineering, Data, ML/AI, DevOps, Security, Product, etc.
seniority_level IC / Senior / Staff / Principal / Manager / Director.
primary_stack Languages and frameworks (e.g., Python, Go, React, PyTorch, Kubernetes).
must_have_skills Non-negotiable skills or experiences.
nice_to_have_skills Preferences that expand but don’t restrict the pool.
target_locations Cities / regions; remote constraints (time zones, countries).
company_archetypes Ideal and acceptable employer types and verticals.
avoid_list Companies or types to avoid (e.g., conflicts, non-competes).

5.2 Key Signals Used in Sourcing Patterns

  • Title synonyms: “ML Engineer”, “Applied Scientist”, “Research Engineer”, etc.
  • Stack synonyms: “ML Ops” vs “MLOps”, “LLM” vs “large language model”.
  • Location radius: city vs metro vs time zone-based remote.
  • Company tiers: direct competitors, adjacent companies, infra providers, and “hidden gem” startups.
  • Channel fit: LinkedIn vs GitHub vs niche communities.
Note: Sourcing A[i]gent is not trying to guess culture fit; it’s creating a clear, shared “where to look and what to look for” starting point for humans.

6. Search Strategy & Boolean Patterns

The agent generates search patterns for the channels recruiters use most often today, starting with LinkedIn, GitHub, and Google X-ray of public profiles.

6.1 LinkedIn Boolean – Overview

For LinkedIn, patterns combine:

  • Title cluster (current and past titles).
  • Skill cluster (languages, frameworks, tools).
  • Domain keywords (e.g., LLMs, distributed systems, observability).
  • Negative filters (e.g., “intern”, “student”, “bootcamp”).

6.2 Example – Senior Backend Engineer (Python, Distributed Systems)

Component Example Snippet
Titles ("backend engineer" OR "back-end engineer" OR "software engineer" OR "backend developer")
Languages ("Python" OR "Go" OR "Golang")
Domain ("distributed systems" OR "microservices" OR "scalability" OR "high-throughput")
Negatives NOT ("intern" OR "student" OR "bootcamp")

Combined Boolean example:

("backend engineer" OR "back-end engineer" OR "software engineer")
AND ("Python" OR "Go" OR "Golang")
AND ("distributed systems" OR "microservices" OR "high scale" OR "high-throughput")
NOT ("intern" OR "student" OR "bootcamp")

6.3 GitHub Search – Overview

For GitHub, patterns focus on repos and profiles that show evidence of relevant skills:

  • Languages and frameworks (e.g., language:Python).
  • Keywords in README / code (e.g., “LLM”, “Kubernetes”, “PyTorch”).
  • Signals of engagement (e.g., stars, forks, contributions).

6.4 Example – ML Engineer (LLMs)

("LLM" OR "large language model" OR "transformers")
("PyTorch" OR "TensorFlow" OR "JAX")
language:Python
stars:>10

6.5 Google X-Ray of LinkedIn Profiles

For open web search, patterns typically look like:

site:linkedin.com/in
("Senior Machine Learning Engineer" OR "Applied Scientist" OR "ML Engineer")
("LLM" OR "large language model" OR "RLHF" OR "transformer")
("San Francisco" OR "Bay Area" OR "San Jose" OR "Oakland")
-"student" -"intern"

All patterns are meant to be starting points. Recruiters are encouraged to tweak and save variants that perform well as templates in the sourcing library.

7. Company & Market Mapping

Strong tech sourcing is not just about individuals; it’s about understanding the ecosystems where they work. Sourcing A[i]gent uses a simple, structured model for company mapping.

7.1 Company Tiers

Tier Description Examples (for AI / ML Roles)
Tier 1 – Direct Competitors Core AI labs and companies solving similar problems for similar users. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Cohere, xAI
Tier 2 – Adjacent & Infra Applied AI, infra, and tooling companies with overlapping skills and domains. Scale AI, Databricks, Snowflake, Hugging Face, W&B
Tier 3 – Hidden Gems Mid-stage startups, open-source teams, and niche verticals with relevant tech. Series B–D AI startups, strong OSS-heavy teams in fintech / healthtech / devtools

7.2 Company Index Structure

A simple company index can be stored in a sheet or database with fields like:

  • company_name
  • parent_company
  • group / holding company
  • tier (1 / 2 / 3)
  • primary_vertical (ads, infra, fintech, games, etc.)
  • stack_tags (Python, Rust, Kubernetes, etc.)

7.3 Using the Company Map with HMs

  • Review Tier 1–3 lists in intake to confirm “on-target”, “stretch”, and “no-go” companies.
  • Align on how aggressive to be with direct competitors.
  • Identify at least one “backup market” (e.g., devtools infra if core AI labs are saturated).

8. Calibration & Governance

Like the screening rubric, sourcing patterns and market maps should evolve as the team learns what works.

8.1 Shadow Mode & A/B Patterns

  • Run Sourcing A[i]gent patterns alongside existing “manual” patterns for several roles.
  • Track which patterns produce more qualified responses and screens.
  • Promote high-performing patterns into the shared template library.

8.2 HM Feedback Loops

  • Use early candidate examples to validate the role template and target companies.
  • Update must-have / nice-to-have lists based on real conversations, not just the initial JD.
  • Document pattern updates after each intake / recalibration meeting.

8.3 Governance & Access

  • Store role templates and company index in a shared, version-controlled location.
  • Limit edit rights for core taxonomies (tiers, verticals) to TA Ops.
  • Encourage recruiters to propose new patterns via pull requests or simple change requests.

9. Integration & Handoffs

Sourcing A[i]gent is most powerful when it feeds directly into Screening, Workflow, and Metrics A[i]gents.

9.1 Example Fields / Artifacts

Artifact / Field Used By Purpose
role_profile_id All A[i]gents Connects sourcing, screening, and interview workflows for the same role.
sourcing_boolean_linkedin Recruiters Primary LinkedIn search pattern used in sourcing sprints.
sourcing_boolean_xray Recruiters Google X-ray pattern for public profiles.
sourcing_pattern_github Recruiters GitHub code / repo search pattern for engineering roles.
target_companies_tier1_3 Recruiters & HMs Shared list of Tier 1–3 companies to focus on.
estimated_pool_size Metrics A[i]gent Rough sizing (small / medium / large) for pipeline planning.

9.2 Handoff to Screening A[i]gent

  • Role templates inform Screening rubrics (what “relevant experience” and “stack match” look like).
  • Company index tiers inform Employer Fit scoring and talent mapping.
  • Shared IDs help track candidates from first touch through offers.

10. Metrics & Metrics A[i]gent

Sourcing A[i]gent provides structured inputs that Metrics A[i]gent can use to analyze top-of-funnel health and sourcing effectiveness.

10.1 Key Sourcing Metrics

  • Prospects contacted per week (by role, recruiter, and channel).
  • Warm response rate (reply / interest) by channel and pattern.
  • Prospect → Screened candidate conversion by role template.
  • Screened → Onsite / Offer conversion for each sourcing pattern.
  • Distribution of hires by company tier and sourcing channel.

10.2 Event Mapping Examples

Sourcing Event Metrics Field Used For
sourcing_pattern_id sourcing_pattern_id Attribution of candidates and hires to specific patterns.
prospects_contacted prospects_contacted Volume and activity tracking per role / recruiter.
prospects_interested prospects_interested Warm response rate and channel effectiveness.
source_channel source_channel Channel mix (LinkedIn, referral, events, inbound, etc.).
hire_source_pattern hire_source_pattern Which patterns and markets actually generate hires.

Over time, this allows TA leaders to say, “For Staff ML roles, our best hires came from Tier 2 infra companies using Pattern B, not from Tier 1 AI labs using Pattern A” — and adjust sourcing strategy accordingly.

Appendix A – Example Tech Role Profiles

These examples show how Sourcing A[i]gent might frame different tech roles at a high level.

A.1 Senior Backend Engineer (Python / Distributed Systems)

  • Titles: Senior Software Engineer, Senior Backend Engineer.
  • Stack: Python, Go, microservices, distributed systems, cloud (AWS/GCP).
  • Companies: Product-led SaaS, infra tooling, devtools, high-scale consumer apps.
  • Channels: LinkedIn, GitHub, engineering meetups / communities.

A.2 Staff ML Engineer (LLMs)

  • Titles: Staff ML Engineer, Applied Scientist, Senior Research Engineer.
  • Stack: Python, PyTorch / JAX, LLMs, MLOps, GPU optimization.
  • Companies: AI labs, applied AI, infra / tooling providers, OSS-heavy teams.
  • Channels: LinkedIn, GitHub, arXiv, ML communities / conferences.

A.3 Data Engineer (Analytics & Pipelines)

  • Titles: Data Engineer, Analytics Engineer, Platform Data Engineer.
  • Stack: SQL, dbt, Airflow, Spark, warehouses (Snowflake, BigQuery).
  • Companies: Product analytics-heavy SaaS, marketplaces, fintech, infra.
  • Channels: LinkedIn, dbt community, data Slack groups.

A.4 DevOps / Platform Engineer

  • Titles: DevOps Engineer, Site Reliability Engineer, Platform Engineer.
  • Stack: Kubernetes, Terraform, CI/CD, observability (Prometheus, Grafana).
  • Companies: Infra-heavy SaaS, devtools, platform teams inside product companies.
  • Channels: LinkedIn, GitHub, DevOps communities, CNCF ecosystem events.
