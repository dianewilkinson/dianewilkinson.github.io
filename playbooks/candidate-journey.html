1. Overview & Outcomes

1.1 Why a Candidate Journey Playbook?

Most companies have a rough idea of their “candidate journey,” but very few have a single, shared view of:

  • What candidates should experience at each stage,
  • What messages they should receive (and when),
  • Which steps are automated vs. human-driven, and
  • Who actually owns each touchpoint.

This playbook creates that shared source of truth so that recruiters, coordinators, hiring managers, and operations are all running the same play.

1.2 Outcomes We’re Aiming For

  • Predictable, transparent candidate experience from first touch through onboarding.
  • Minimal “black box” moments where candidates don’t know what’s happening.
  • Clear automation opportunities that save time without sacrificing warmth.
  • Fewer one-off emails; more reusable templates and workflows.
  • Better data for Metrics A[i]gent to analyze bottlenecks and sentiment.

2. Journey Stages

The journey is broken into six core stages. Some candidates will skip stages (e.g., internal moves); others may loop within stages (multiple panels), but the mental model stays consistent.

# Stage Primary Goal Typical Owner
0 Awareness & Interest Make the company and role feel relevant and worth exploring. Employer Brand, Marketing, Talent Brand, TA
1 Application / Sourcing Capture interest and gather enough information for screening. Recruiters, Sourcing, Screening A[i]gent
2 Initial Contact & Screen Confirm basics, mutual fit, and motivation. Recruiter / Talent Partner
3 Hiring Manager & Panel Interviews Assess skills, behaviours, and mutual expectations. Hiring Manager, Interviewers, Workflow A[i]gent
4 Offer & Close Align on details and secure a confident “yes.” Recruiter, HM, Comp / People Ops
5 Pre-Start & Onboarding Transition from “candidate” to “teammate” smoothly. Recruiting, HR/People Ops, Manager

Each stage has its own set of touchpoints, message types, and automations. The next sections detail those patterns.

3. Core Touchpoints & Message Types

For each stage, we define the minimum viable touchpoints that should exist for most roles. Additional role-specific messages can layer on top of this baseline.

3.1 Awareness & Interest

  • Job postings and career site pages with clear expectations and process overview.
  • LinkedIn / outbound messages that are personalised but templatized.
  • Role one-pagers or FAQ links for senior/critical roles.

3.2 Application / Sourcing

  • Application confirmation – immediate, automated, with timeline expectations.
  • “We received your application” status page – basic transparency.
  • Early disqualification responses – respectful, quick, and clear.

3.3 Initial Contact & Screen

  • Availability request + brief outline of screen agenda.
  • Calendar invite with context, links, and interviewer name.
  • Post-screen follow-up: next steps or polite close-out with optional feedback.

3.4 Hiring Manager & Panel Interviews

  • Stage-specific prep email (“What to expect in the panel”).
  • Reminder email 24 hours before interviews.
  • Same-day logistics reminder for complex panels / onsite loops.
  • Post-interview update: decision, timeline, or “still reviewing” note.

3.5 Offer & Close

  • Offer call scheduling + high-level overview.
  • Written offer package and recap of role, team, and expectations.
  • “Decision support” follow-up: FAQs, benefits, equity explainers.

3.6 Pre-Start & Onboarding

  • Welcome note after acceptance with next-step checklist.
  • Pre-start check-ins (1–2 touchpoints depending on lead time).
  • Day-one logistics and team introduction note.
Design principle: every “wait” in the process should have a corresponding communication, even if the message is “we’re still reviewing.”

4. Automation vs Human Touchpoints

The goal is not to automate everything; it is to automate the predictable pieces so recruiters can spend energy on judgement and relationship-building.

4.1 Good Candidates for Automation

  • Application confirmations and basic status updates.
  • Availability requests and calendar reminders.
  • Standard delay / “we’re still here” messages.
  • Interview reminders and thank-you notes.

4.2 Places to Stay Human-Led

  • Initial outreach to high-priority or executive candidates.
  • Nuanced feedback conversations and rejections late in the process.
  • Offer calls, negotiation, and major expectation resets.
  • Any time trust is at risk or context is complex.

4.3 How This Connects to A[i]gents

  • Screening A[i]gent feeds structured recommendations into the “Initial Contact & Screen” stage.
  • Workflow A[i]gent drives most interview confirmations, reminders, and delay updates.
  • Metrics A[i]gent observes time-in-stage, communication coverage, and drop-off points.

5. SLAs, Guardrails & Risk

SLAs keep the journey moving and make it clear when something is off track. Guardrails ensure automations don’t accidentally degrade experience.

5.1 Example SLAs

  • Application → first review: within 3 business days.
  • Screen → decision: within 2 business days of the interview.
  • Panel → decision: within 5 business days, with at least one update in the meantime.
  • Offer → written doc: within 24 hours of verbal yes.

5.2 Guardrails

  • No automated messages that contradict each other (e.g., two different statuses).
  • Templates are reviewed quarterly for tone, inclusivity, and clarity.
  • Recruiters can pause automations for sensitive or escalated candidates.
  • All automated communications are visible in the ATS timeline.

5.3 Risks & Mitigations

  • Risk: Candidates feel like they’re talking to a robot.
    Mitigation: Use warm, specific language; mix in live touches at key inflection points.
  • Risk: Automations fire on out-of-date data.
    Mitigation: Use clear stage-based triggers; pilot changes before global rollout.
  • Risk: SLAs are unrealistic.
    Mitigation: Set SLAs with real recruiter bandwidth in mind and revisit quarterly.

6. Metrics & Feedback Loops

A journey is only as good as its measurement. Metrics A[i]gent uses structured events from Screening and Workflow A[i]gents to monitor how the candidate journey is actually functioning.

6.1 Core Journey Metrics

  • Time-in-stage (median and percentiles) for each stage.
  • Drop-off rate by stage (who stops engaging and where).
  • Communication coverage – % of candidates who received the expected touchpoints.
  • Candidate satisfaction / NPS where surveys exist.

6.2 Feedback Sources

  • Candidate surveys linked in closing or rejection emails.
  • Hiring manager feedback on perceived clarity and speed.
  • Recruiter input during retros for critical roles.

6.3 How This Loops Back Into Design

  • Stages with long time-in-stage and low comms coverage are top priorities for Workflow A[i]gent.
  • Drop-off analysis informs message content and channel (e.g., SMS vs. email for reminders).
  • Qualitative feedback guides template tone and expectation-setting tweaks.

Appendix – Example Message Library

Below are shortened examples of messages for key stages. In practice, each of these would live as templates inside your ATS or communication tool, with variants by office/role/seniority.

A.1 Application Confirmation

Subject: Thanks for applying for [Role] at [Company]
Body (excerpt): “Thanks so much for your interest in [Company] and the [Role] role. Our team reviews every application. You can expect an update within [X] business days. In the meantime, here’s what the process typically looks like…”

A.2 Screen Scheduling

Subject: Quick intro chat – [Role] at [Company]
Body (excerpt): “I’d love to connect for a short intro conversation to learn more about your background and share context on the role. Here’s a link to my calendar, or reply with a few times that work for you…”

A.3 “We’re Still Here” During Panel Review

Subject: Quick update on your interviews with [Company]
Body (excerpt): “Thanks again for taking the time to meet with the team. We’re still consolidating feedback and expect to have next steps by [date]. I’ll reach out as soon as we have a decision, and please don’t hesitate to reply here if anything changes on your side.”

A.4 Respectful Close-Out

Subject: Update on your application for [Role]
Body (excerpt): “After careful review, we’ve decided not to move forward for this specific role. This wasn’t an easy decision, and it reflects the match against this opening rather than your overall experience. We truly appreciate the time you invested and wish you the best in your search…”

These examples are intentionally short and plain-spoken; tone and specifics can be tuned to match your brand voice and regional norms.
