1. Source Quality Snapshot

Below is a simplified view of how many candidates you need from each source to make one hire for a particular role family (numbers illustrative but realistic for tech / GTM).

Example: for this role, it takes roughly 250 inbound applications, 40 sourced candidates, or 20 referrals to make one hire.
Source Approx. Candidates per Hire Signal
Inbound (job boards / careers site) ≈ 250 Very high volume, low average signal.
Sourced (outbound) ≈ 40 Curated, higher intent after contact.
Referrals ≈ 20 Highest quality and fastest path to hire.

The exact numbers will vary by company and role, but the pattern is common: inbound dominates volume, while referrals and outbound dominate quality and conversion.

2. How to Read This as a TA Ops Leader

2.1 Inbound is noisy by default

Inbound tends to be the easiest source to “grow” (post more jobs, open more apply links, add more boards), but the marginal value of each extra inbound applicant is small.

  • Reviewing 250+ resumes per hire is expensive in recruiter time.
  • Without structure, it also increases inconsistency and bias.
  • It can hide the fact that better sources are quietly doing most of the work.

2.2 Sourced and referrals are high-leverage

Even if sourced and referrals are “harder” to generate, the conversion math is different:

  • 40 sourced candidates → 1 hire often means deeper research but far better alignment.
  • 20 referrals → 1 hire shows that engaged employees are pre-filtering for fit.
  • Every incremental sourced / referral candidate is worth more than another random inbound apply.

2.3 The mix tells you how to reallocate time

If your data looks like this and your team is still spending most of its week triaging inbound, there is an obvious optimization opportunity:

  • Automate more of the inbound review (Screening A[i]gent).
  • Move recruiter time toward outbound, referral programs, and high-touch work.
  • Use Workflow A[i]gent to keep high-quality candidates moving quickly.

3. Strategy & Budget Implications

3.1 Where to spend recruiter time

  • Dial back manual resume review for low-signal inbound channels.
  • Set minimum outbound activity targets where sourced hires outperform inbound.
  • Give recruiters capacity for deeper intake, calibration, and close work.

3.2 Where to spend money

  • Job board spend should be justified against actual hires, not just clicks or applies.
  • Referral bonuses and referral enablement tools often pay for themselves quickly.
  • Specialist tools (e.g., for targeted sourcing) can be worth more than another general job board.

3.3 How to talk about this with leadership

“For this role, one hire takes ~250 inbound applicants, ~40 sourced candidates, or ~20 referrals. If we want to hire faster with better signal, we should invest in the channels that convert — and automate more of the inbound noise.”

4. Metrics A[i]gent & Ongoing Monitoring

In a full implementation, these insights become part of standard reporting rather than a one-off analysis. Metrics A[i]gent would track, by role and time period:

  • Candidates per hire by source (inbound / outbound / referrals / internal).
  • Stage-by-stage conversion by source (screen pass, interview pass, offer rate).
  • Time-to-fill differences by source mix.
  • Cost-per-hire and recruiter time-per-hire by source.

Those metrics feed directly into decisions about:

  • Which channels to scale up or down.
  • Which recruiters are particularly strong in outbound vs. inbound-heavy roles.
  • Where candidate experience investments will have the biggest payoff.

5. Recommended Actions

For a TA leader looking at a chart like this, a simple action plan might be:

  1. Baseline the data. Run a 6–12 month lookback by role family to confirm your own candidates-per-hire by source.
  2. Set a target mix. For example: “At least 40% of hires from referrals + outbound for AE roles next quarter.”
  3. Free up time. Use Screening A[i]gent and basic automations to reduce manual inbound review.
  4. Invest in high-quality channels. Referral programs, targeted sourcing, and better intake.
  5. Review quarterly. Use Metrics A[i]gent dashboards to see if the mix and conversion are improving — and iterate.

This is a small example, but it shows the kind of story you can tell when recruiting data is clean, structured, and connected to decision-making — exactly the kind of work this portfolio is designed to highlight.
